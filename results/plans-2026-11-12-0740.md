> A mum in her 50s whose son died by suicide six months ago. She's not sleeping, can't face friends, and says general bereavement groups "don't understand". She can drive. What support is there?

**Reading:** A mum whose son died by suicide six months ago, now isolated and not sleeping. She wants support from people who share that experience, so I put suicide-bereavement services first and kept the wider wellbeing options gentle and low-pressure.

**Safety:** If the pain ever feels too much, Samaritans are there any time on 116 123, and in immediate danger call 999. You don't have to be at crisis point to call.

1. **Somerset Suicide Bereavement Support Service** (First step; phone/online or area-wide; Mon-Sun 00:00-23:59)  
   A free 24/7 helpline, with support workers and counselling, for people affected by a death by suicide. She can self-refer, and it is available at night when she can't sleep.
2. **Speak Their Name Group (Mendip)** (First step; Shepton Mallet; monthly (dates vary))  
   A local peer group led by staff with lived experience of suicide loss, so she is with people who understand. Self-referral lets her ask about venue and dates first.
3. **SoBS Online Forum** (Next step; phone/online or area-wide; no time)  
   An online forum for adults bereaved by suicide. She can read or post at any hour, including sleepless nights, without facing anyone in person.
4. **SoBS National Virtual Support Group** (Next step; phone/online or area-wide; Tue 3rd Tue of month)  
   A Zoom group for people bereaved by suicide, run by trained volunteers who have been through it. She can join from home, and email or call first for the link.
5. **SoBS Supportline and Email Support** (Next step; phone/online or area-wide; no time)  
   A listening line and email support from volunteers who are themselves bereaved by suicide. It is not counselling, but it is a gentle way to talk to someone who understands.
6. **Suicide Bereavement Peer Walk** (When ready; phone/online or area-wide; dates vary)  
   A nature walk for adults bereaved by suicide with no need to talk. It suits someone not ready for a sit-down group, and she can drive to the venue.
7. **Health Walk: Shepton Mallet Tuesday** (When ready; 0.2 mi; Tue 10:00)  
   A gentle, short, flat walk in Shepton Mallet with no pressure to talk. Fresh air and light activity can help low mood and sleep, and it is free to join.

**Gaps:** Nothing on the list is aimed specifically at sleep problems after bereavement, so her GP is worth a conversation. NHS Somerset Talking Therapies excludes bereavement in the last 6 months, so she should check whether she now qualifies.

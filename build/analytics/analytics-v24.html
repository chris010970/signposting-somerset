How the Analytics tab works

  • What it shows: each small census area (LSOA, about 1,500 people) beside the support recorded nearby in this directory. It's for exploring, not a measure of need.
  • Ranking: by the indicator you choose, never by distance: the national deprivation or health rank from the English Indices of Deprivation 2025 (1 = most deprived of 33,755 in England), the share of residents disabled (Census 2021), or people per km² (least dense first). Ties go to the larger population. The grid groups areas into five bands of the chosen indicator (rows) and five distance bands (columns), each holding a fifth of residents by distance to the nearest matching support, so the miles change with Support for. Each square has its own colour, and the map uses the same colours.
  • Distances: straight lines from where people live in each area (the average position of its postcodes), not travel times. The search area is 1 mile in towns and 3 miles in rural areas (ONS urban/rural classification 2021).
  • What it can't tell you: how much a service can take, how good it is, or whether it's easy to reach. "None recorded" may mean a gap in this directory rather than in local provision.
  • Data: ONS boundaries, population (mid-2022) and urban/rural classification; MHCLG Indices of Deprivation 2025; Census 2021; open postcode data (2016). Open Government Licence.

How Analytics works

  • What it shows: each small census area (an LSOA, about 1,500 people) and how far its residents live from the nearest in-person support of the kind you choose. It's for exploring where provision may be thin, not a measure of need.
  • Which support: one kind at a time, from those where being there in person matters most: connecting with others, being active, low mood, anxiety, bereavement, dementia, caring for someone, addiction, food, money, housing, and crisis support. Learning, volunteering, and specialist support with only a handful of in-person services aren't included. How often narrows it to services that meet most days, at least weekly, or fortnightly or monthly.
  • The chart: rows are five bands of the indicator you choose under Compare areas by: deprivation or health deprivation (English Indices of Deprivation 2025), the share of residents disabled (Census 2021), or people per km². Columns are fifths of residents by distance to the nearest matching support, so each column holds about the same number of people, and its miles change with the kind of support. Each square's colour is used for its areas on the map.
  • The list: areas ranked by the indicator, never by distance (1 = most deprived of 33,755 in England); ties go to the larger population.
  • Distances: straight lines from where people live in each area (the average position of its postcodes), not travel times. The local profile also counts support within 1 mile in towns and 3 miles in rural areas (ONS urban/rural classification 2021).
  • What it can't tell you: how much a service can take, how good it is, or how easy it is to reach. "None recorded" may mean a gap in this directory rather than in local provision.
  • Data: ONS boundaries, population (mid-2022) and urban/rural classification; MHCLG Indices of Deprivation 2025; Census 2021; open postcode data (2016). Open Government Licence.
